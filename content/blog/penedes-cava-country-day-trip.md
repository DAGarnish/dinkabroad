Here's something most visitors to [Coma-ruga](/blog/coma-ruga-guide-for-pickleball-players) don't realise: they're already in wine country. Coma-ruga belongs to El Vendrell, the capital of the **Baix Penedès**, and the vineyards of the Penedès, Catalonia's most famous wine region, roll inland from the coast. A day among the vines is one of the best ways to spend a rest day on a pickleball trip.

## What is the Penedès?

The Penedès is a wine region between Barcelona and Tarragona, stretching from the Mediterranean up to the hills below Montserrat. It's best known as the home of **cava**, Spain's traditional-method sparkling wine, but it also makes excellent still wines.

The region is built on three native white grapes:

- **Xarel·lo**, full and structured, the backbone of the best cavas.
- **Macabeu**, fresh and gently floral.
- **Parellada**, delicate and aromatic, grown higher up where it's cooler.

You'll also find Garnatxa, Malvasia, Muscat and a growing number of recovered local varieties, and organic farming is widespread.

## Sant Sadurní d'Anoia: the cava capital

Inland, **Sant Sadurní d'Anoia** is the heart of cava production. The town sits on top of kilometres of underground cellars where bottles age for months or years before release. Some of the big cava houses offer tours of their caves, often by little train, followed by a tasting. It's a fun, easy introduction to how cava is made.

## Vilafranca del Penedès: the wine capital

**Vilafranca del Penedès** is the region's main town and a lovely place to wander, with arcaded streets, a busy Saturday market and **VINSEUM**, a museum dedicated to the culture of wine. Vilafranca is also famous for its *castellers*, the teams who build Catalonia's human towers, and its festival at the end of August is one of the best places in the region to see them.

## Small family wineries

For many visitors, the highlight of the Penedès is visiting a **small family winery**: walking the vines with the people who farm them and tasting wines you'll never find at home. Most small estates open by appointment only, so book ahead.

The coastal hills of the **Massís del Garraf**, between Sitges and Coma-ruga, are home to some of the most characterful producers. Shallow limestone soils and the sea breeze give their wines a distinctive minerality and salty finish. ## How to get around

The Penedès is easy to reach but spread out, and Spain's drink-driving limits are strict. Your options:

1. **Train to Vilafranca or Sant Sadurní.** Both towns are on the railway, and some cava houses are walkable from the station.
2. **A tour with transport.** The simplest way to visit smaller estates, which are often off the beaten track.
3. **A designated driver.** If you hire a car, someone in the group doesn't taste.

## When to go

The vineyards are beautiful year-round, but **autumn** is special. The harvest runs from late August into October, and afterwards the vines turn gold and red. Spring is lovely too, with fresh green growth and mild weather.

## Tips for tasting

- **Pace yourself.** Two wineries in a day is plenty.
- **Eat as you go.** Many wineries serve local cheese, cured meats and *pa amb tomàquet* alongside the wines.
- **Ship it home.** Ask the winery about shipping if you fall in love with a bottle that won't fit in your hand luggage.
