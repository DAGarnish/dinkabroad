Pickleball has gone from a back-garden curiosity to the sport everyone you know seems to be playing. Once you're hooked, the next question is obvious: where can you play somewhere warm? Spain is the natural answer for players from the UK and Europe. It's a short flight away, the sun is reliable for much of the year, and there's a growing pickleball scene along the Catalan coast.

If you've never been on a pickleball holiday before, here's what one looks like, using our five-day Local Dink retreat in Coma-ruga as the example.

## Mornings on court, afternoons off

The best pickleball holidays don't try to cram eight hours of play into every day. The rhythm that works is simple:

- **Mornings** are for pickleball: coaching, drills, match play and, at the end of the trip, a tournament.
- **Afternoons** are yours. Lie on the beach, swim, sit by the pool, or join a relaxed outing along the coast.

That balance matters more than you'd think. You'll play better on day four if you haven't spent every afternoon in the midday heat.

## A typical five days

Here's how Local Dink runs, Friday to Tuesday:

1. **Friday: arrive and warm up.** Travel to Coma-ruga, about an hour along the coast by train from Barcelona. Check in, meet the group and get some open play in during the afternoon.
2. **Saturday: first coaching session.** A 90-minute session with certified coaches, followed by open play. In the afternoon, take the train to Sitges with your host or stay on the beach.
3. **Sunday: second coaching session.** More coaching, then plenty of match play against different opponents. In the afternoon, visit Roman Tarragona.
4. **Monday: tournament day.** A fun, competitive tournament with your fellow travellers and local players. Spend the afternoon in Reus or by the sea.
5. **Tuesday: open play and home.** A final breakfast by the sea and optional open play before you head home.

## What level do you need to be?

Any level. Groups include players who've only just picked up a paddle as well as experienced 4.0+ players. Good coaching on a trip like this is grouped by level, so a beginner isn't lost and a strong player isn't bored.

If you're newer to the game, the coaching sessions are where you'll make the biggest jump. Two focused 90-minute sessions with a certified coach can fix habits that would take months to unpick on your own.

## Can you come on your own?

Yes, and a lot of people do. Small groups (we cap ours at 16 players) mean you're never short of playing partners, and by the second evening most solo travellers have found a dinner table. If you're booking alone, a single room is available.

## What to pack

- **Your paddle,** plus a spare if you have one. Paddles travel fine in hand luggage on most airlines, but check your carrier's rules before you fly.
- **Court shoes.** Proper court shoes, not running trainers, as your ankles will thank you.
- **Sun protection.** A cap or visor, sunglasses and high-factor sun cream, even outside summer.
- **Light layers** for the evenings in spring and autumn.
- **Swimwear,** obviously.

You won't need balls. They're provided on court.

## Getting there

Fly into Barcelona, then take the train down the coast. The direct train from central Barcelona to Sant Vicenç de Calders, the station for Coma-ruga, takes around an hour and runs along the Garraf coast, with the sea on one side most of the way. It's one of the nicer train journeys in Spain.
