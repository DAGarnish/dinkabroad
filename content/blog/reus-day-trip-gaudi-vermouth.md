Most people who visit the Costa Daurada head for the beaches. Fewer make it a few kilometres inland to **Reus**, and that's a shame. This handsome market town is the home town of Antoni Gaudí, has one of the richest collections of modernista architecture in Catalonia, and takes its vermouth very seriously. It's a perfect slow afternoon away from the courts, and one of the hosted outings on our retreat in [Coma-ruga](/blog/coma-ruga-guide-for-pickleball-players).

## Getting there

Reus sits just inland from Tarragona and is easy to reach from the coast. On our Local Dink retreat, your host handles the route there and back, so all you need to do is turn up.

## Gaudí's home town

Antoni Gaudí, the architect behind Barcelona's Sagrada Família, was born in Reus in 1852 and grew up here. Curiously, he never built anything in his home town, but Reus celebrates him anyway at the **Gaudí Centre**, a modern museum on the main square, the **Plaça del Mercadal**. It explores his life, his ideas and his inspirations from nature, and it's a great primer if you're heading to Barcelona afterwards.

You can also follow a walking route past places connected with his childhood.

## The modernista route

Reus made its fortune in the 19th century, and its wealthy merchants commissioned some of the best architects of the modernista era. The result is a town full of extraordinary buildings, many of them on the **Ruta del Modernisme**, a walking route marked out through the centre.

The star is **Casa Navàs** on the Plaça del Mercadal, designed by **Lluís Domènech i Montaner**, the architect of Barcelona's Palau de la Música Catalana. Its interior, still with much of its original decoration and furniture, is one of the most complete modernista homes anywhere. Domènech i Montaner also designed the **Institut Pere Mata**, a remarkable psychiatric hospital on the edge of town.

## Vermouth, the Reus way

Reus has a long history with **vermouth**. In the 19th and early 20th centuries it was a major producer and exporter, and the drink is still part of everyday life here.

Do as the locals do and go **"fer el vermut"**: a pre-lunch vermouth, usually served over ice with an olive and a slice of orange, alongside olives, crisps and anchovies or other small bites. The town's **Museu del Vermut** tells the story and has a bar where you can try plenty of them.

## Shopping and the old town

Reus is one of the best shopping towns in the region, with independent shops, fashion boutiques and food stores packed into the pedestrian streets of the old town. Look out for the tall bell tower of the **Prioral de Sant Pere** church, which rises above the rooftops.

## Eating in Reus

Lunch is the main meal here, and many restaurants offer a good-value *menú del dia* on weekdays. Look out for Catalan classics such as **romesco** sauce from nearby Tarragona, slow-cooked stews and, in late winter and spring, **calçots**, a type of sweet green onion grilled over flames and dipped in sauce.

## Tips for your visit

- **Go on a weekday afternoon,** when the shops are open and the town is buzzing.
- **Book Casa Navàs in advance.** Visits are by guided tour.
- **Time it for vermouth hour,** from around midday before lunch.
