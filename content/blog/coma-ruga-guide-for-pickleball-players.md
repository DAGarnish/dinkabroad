Most people who visit Catalonia never get further down the coast than Sitges. That's their loss. An hour south of Barcelona, Coma-ruga offers long sandy beaches, palm-lined promenades and a relaxed Catalan pace, without the city crowds. It's also where we run our pickleball retreats, so we've got to know it well.

## Where is Coma-ruga?

Coma-ruga is a seaside town in the municipality of El Vendrell, on the **Costa Daurada**, the "Golden Coast" that runs south from the Garraf towards Tarragona. It sits roughly halfway between Barcelona and Tarragona, which makes it an ideal base for exploring both.

## Getting there from Barcelona

Take the direct train from central Barcelona to **Sant Vicenç de Calders**, the station that serves Coma-ruga. The journey takes about an hour and hugs the coastline for much of the way, through Castelldefels, Garraf and Sitges. From the station it's a short taxi ride to the seafront.

## The beach

Coma-ruga's main draw is its beach: a long, wide stretch of golden sand with shallow, generally calm water, backed by a palm-lined promenade. It's the kind of beach where you can walk for half an hour at the water's edge, which is exactly what tired legs need after a morning of drilling third-shot drops.

## Between games

Pickleball takes up the mornings. Here's how to spend the rest of the day without leaving town:

- **Swim and recover.** The sea is warm enough for swimming from late spring to early autumn.
- **Walk the promenade** at sunset, when the whole town seems to come out.
- **Eat like a local.** Look for seafood rice dishes, grilled fish and *pa amb tomàquet*, and remember that Catalans eat late. Dinner before 8.30pm is early.
- **Visit the Pau Casals connection.** The great cellist Pau Casals was born in El Vendrell, and his former seaside home at Sant Salvador, just along the coast, is now a museum.

## Day trips along the coast

Coma-ruga's position on the coast is its secret weapon. All of these are easy half-day trips, and three of them are built into our Local Dink retreat as hosted afternoon outings:

### Tarragona

Around 30 km south, Tarragona was one of the most important cities of Roman Spain, and its Roman remains are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The amphitheatre sits right above the sea, and the old town is full of Roman walls, medieval streets and good places for a vermouth.

### Sitges

Up the coast towards Barcelona, Sitges is the glamorous neighbour: whitewashed streets, a seafront church, a lively bar scene and a string of beaches. Go in the late afternoon and stay for dinner.

### Reus

Inland from Tarragona, Reus is a handsome market town known for its modernista architecture, its strong links to Antoni Gaudí, and its long tradition of vermouth. It's a great place for a slow afternoon of wandering and tapas.

### The Penedès

Catalonia's cava country starts just inland. Small family wineries and big cava houses alike offer tastings, and the vineyards are beautiful in autumn.

## When to go

The Costa Daurada gets plenty of sunshine, and spring and autumn are the sweet spot for pickleball: warm enough for the beach, without the full heat of July and August. Mornings on court stay comfortable for most of the year.
